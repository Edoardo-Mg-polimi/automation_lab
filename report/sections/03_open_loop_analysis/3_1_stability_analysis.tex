\subsection{Stability analysis}

The open-loop poles are obtained from the eigenvalues of the state matrix $A$:
\begin{equation}
    \lambda_i = \mathrm{eig}(A).
\end{equation}

For the extended model, the eigenvalues are
\begin{equation}
    \lambda(A) =
    \left\{
        +52.94,\;
        -52.94,\;
        -529.42
    \right\}\ \mathrm{rad/s}.
    \label{eq:open_loop_poles}
\end{equation}

The pair $\pm 52.94\ \mathrm{rad/s}$ is associated with the mechanical subsystem
\begin{equation}
    G_x(s) = \frac{k_2}{s^2-k_1},
\end{equation}
whose poles are located at $\pm \sqrt{k_1}$. The positive pole is a direct consequence of the
open-loop magnetic levitation dynamics: if the ball moves away from the equilibrium point, the
magnetic force variation does not naturally restore the position. Therefore the equilibrium is unstable
without feedback.

The third pole, $-529.42\ \mathrm{rad/s}$, is introduced by the closed current loop approximation. This
pole is stable and significantly faster than the mechanical unstable pole. Hence, the open-loop
instability of the extended plant is entirely due to the mechanical position dynamics.

Since one eigenvalue has positive real part, the open-loop plant is unstable:
\begin{equation}
    \max_i \Re\{\lambda_i\} = 52.94 > 0.
\end{equation}
This confirms that a position controller is necessary to keep the ball levitated around the selected
equilibrium point.
