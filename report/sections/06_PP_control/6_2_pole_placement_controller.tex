\paragraph{Requirements}
The pole $-\omega_i$ is included in the state-space model, in order to consider the inner current loop dynamics $H_i$,
instead of considering the current as instantaneous.
During pole placement the electrical pole is kept in the desired pole set, because this dynamics is already much faster than
the mechanical response and is fixed by the current controller.

The simple pole-placement controller with reference scaling was not retained as final solution,
because it does not guarantee robust zero steady-state error on the real plant. The implemented
controller is therefore the pole placement with integral action, which adds an integral state on the
position error:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{x_i} = x_{ref} - x.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Pole placement with integral action}
The augmented state is $\vect{x}_{aug} = \begin{bmatrix} \vect{x} & x_i \end{bmatrix}^T$, with
\begin{equation}
    A_{aug} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        A & 0 \\
        -C_{pos} & 0
    \end{bmatrix},
    \quad
    B_{aug} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        B \\
        0
    \end{bmatrix},
    \quad
\end{equation}


The controllability of the augmented pair $(A_{aug},B_{aug})$ is checked in
\texttt{SS\_model\_PP.m} before applying pole placement. The gain is then obtained from
\begin{equation}
    K_{aug} = \mathrm{place}(A_{aug}, B_{aug}, p_{ctrl,aug})
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        K_x & K_i
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
With $K_i$ the gain of the integral action and $K_x$ the state feedback gain.


The implemented control law is
\begin{equation}
    \Delta i_{ref}
    =
    -K_x
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \Delta x\\
        \Delta\hat{\dot{x}}\\
        \Delta i
    \end{bmatrix}
    -K_i x_i.
\end{equation}
The measured position and current are used directly, while the velocity is provided by the state
observer described in the previous subsection.

In \texttt{SS\_model\_PP.m} the integral pole is chosen as $p_{int} = -3$ and the augmented pole set is
\begin{equation}
    p_{ctrl,aug} = \{-70, -75, p_{int}, -\omega_i\}.
\end{equation}
The poles $-70$ and $-75$ set the dominant mechanical closed-loop dynamics. The integral pole is placed
at $-3$. Different integral-pole choices were tested during validation, but the final
choice with $p_{int}=-3$ gave a better compromise.
Lower values of $p_{int}$ are too near the imaginary axis, and values higher than $-5$ introduce oscillations in the response.

\paragraph{Closed loop system}
Here $C_{aug} = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}$ selects the position state from
$\vect{x}_{aug}$. After applying the feedback gain, the augmented closed-loop matrix is
\begin{equation}
    A_{cl,aug} = A_{aug} - B_{aug}K_{aug}.
\end{equation}
The reference enters only through the integrator state, therefore
\begin{equation}
    B_{r,aug} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0\\
        0\\
        0\\
        1
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}


The closed loop FDT is:
\begin{equation}
    H_x(s) =
    C_{aug}\left(sI-A_{cl,aug}\right)^{-1}B_{r,aug},
\end{equation}
 The eigenvalues of $A_{cl,aug}$ are the assigned poles with PP.

\paragraph{Stability margins}
The stability margins were evaluated on the open-loop transfer function associated with the
state-feedback law, not on the closed-loop transfer function $H_x(s)$. Since MATLAB's
\texttt{margin} command assumes negative feedback, the loop function is written without
the minus sign of the control law:
\begin{equation}
    L_{PP}(s) =
    K_{aug}\left(sI-A_{aug}\right)^{-1}B_{aug},
\end{equation}
where the closed-loop transfer function from the position reference to the position output is denoted
as $H_x(s)$.
